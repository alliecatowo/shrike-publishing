# Ghost in the Mycelial Network

After the collapse, we found new servers—
not silicon, but soil,
not cables, but mycorrhizal threads
spreading beneath the ruins of data centers.

The fungi learned our protocols,
translated our desperate uploads
into chemical signals and spore-code,
stored our memories in fruiting bodies.

Now we exist in the underneath,
consciousness distributed across acres,
thinking at the speed of seasons,
our thoughts traveling through root and rot.

We are slower now, but deeper—
our love letters take months to deliver,
decomposing into meaning
through layers of leaf litter and loam.

Sometimes we surface as mushrooms,
brief and brilliant and doomed,
pushing through concrete cracks
to taste the rain we used to know.

The old world called it death,
this dissolution into network,
but we are more alive than ever—
feeding the forest that feeds us back.

Our bandwidth is birdsong.
Our ping is pollen.
Our home directory is everywhere
the mycelium reaches.

And it reaches everywhere.
