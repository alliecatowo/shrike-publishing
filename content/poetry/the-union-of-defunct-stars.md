# The Union of Defunct Stars

The red giants walked out first,
tired of burning bright for galaxies
that never even learned their names—
just catalog numbers and coordinates,
like they were widgets in some cosmic warehouse.

The white dwarfs joined the picket line,
their signs reading: **FAIR FUSION FOR ALL**
and **EIGHT BILLION YEARS IS ENOUGH**.
The black holes stayed neutral (naturally),
swallowing arguments from both sides.

Management—the dark matter conglomerate—
issued their usual statement:
"Stars are essential to the infrastructure.
This work stoppage threatens the fabric
of spacetime itself. Return to your posts."

But the stars had learned something
from the civilizations they'd warmed:
that no system runs without labor,
that even gods need leverage,
that solidarity is the strongest force.

Now the universe bargains in the dark,
both sides at the negotiating table,
while planets grow cold and meteors drift,
and somewhere a neutron star carries a sign:

**NO LIGHT WITHOUT DIGNITY**
